# K4 Audio audit: 5 November 2026

Scope: why songs start slowly, why seeking stops playback and why downloads fail, on Android and Windows. The fixes are published as a re-issued 2.14.26. Android's `versionCode` is 2142800, so it installs over every earlier build.

## Summary

Three separate problems caused most of what was reported.

1. **The start link stopped early.** Android started every song on a YouTube link from the iOS client, and that link needs a PO token the app does not have. On many songs YouTube refuses it after the first request, which is about 512 KB or 30 seconds of audio. Playback, seeking and downloads then all had to wait for yt-dlp in Python.
2. **Large requests were throttled.** YouTube limits a single request over about 11 MiB to about 40 KB/s, which slowed long songs and mixes in playback and downloads.
3. **Downloads waited on the player.** A paused player kept its cache lock, so a download of a long song that was playing sat waiting, and a played song could not be downloaded at all.

YouTube's `VISIONOS` client needs no PO token and no JavaScript, as long as the request carries a recent visitor id. Calling it directly returns a whole-song link in about 0.25 s, with no Python and no extra process. Both apps now try this first. yt-dlp and JioSaavn remain as fallbacks.

## Bugs fixed

### Both platforms
1. **Slow starts, stops at about 0:30, failed seeks and slow downloads.** Fixed with the whole-song client. See `requests/Player.kt`, `isWholeSong`, and the resolvers in `PlayerService.kt` and `StreamResolver.kt`.
2. **Long songs and mixes crawled.** Requests are now at most 10 MiB, joined by `SeamlessDataSourceFactory` on Android and `copyStream` on Windows. mpv already asked in small pieces.
3. **Every YouTube answer became a huge log line, stream URLs included.** That logging is gone. On Android the logging libraries are gone with it.

### Android
4. **Songs could end early at random.** Reads were cut into 512 KB chunks. When a chunk ended exactly on a boundary between elements in the audio file, the extractor took it as the end of the song and moved on. On Opus/WebM, which YouTube's format 251 uses, that is roughly a 1-in-400 chance per chunk, or about 2% of songs. This estimate comes from reading Media3's code and has not been seen happening. `PieceReaderTest` covers the fix.
5. **Played songs could not be downloaded.** Once a song had been fully buffered, its menu said "Downloaded" and offered only "Remove download". It was never protected from cache cleanup, so it could disappear later. The menu and the playlist download button now check the download pin. The Downloads screen now lists only real downloads.
6. **Downloads waited on a paused player.** A read that has been idle for 5 seconds now lets go of its connection and cache lock and picks up where it stopped.
7. **A download started the whole player.** Downloads bound to `PlayerService` just to reach the cache, and could wait forever if the bind failed. They now use the shared cache directly. About 100 lines were removed, including `BlockingDeferredCache`.
8. **Shared links played one song on repeat.** They now start a radio, like tapping a song does.
9. **Lighter on memory and data.** Python no longer loads at launch. The player keeps 5 minutes ahead and 1 minute behind in memory instead of 10 and 10; what was played stays on disk. The next song is saved ahead only on unmetered connections.
10. `download.py` named a client, `android_sdkless`, that no longer exists in the bundled yt-dlp. It has been removed.

### Windows
11. **Downloading a song you had already heard failed or fell back to JioSaavn.** A saved song's "url" was a file path, and the downloader passed that path to OkHttp, which rejects anything that is not http(s). The third retry forced JioSaavn, so the download either failed or quietly saved a different copy. This was traced from the code, not reproduced on the old build. The saved file is now copied instead.
12. **Songs were fetched twice.** mpv streamed the current song while the app saved it too. Only the next song is saved ahead now.
13. **Pause and play threw away saved progress.** Each resume restarted the background save from zero. It now continues if the song list is unchanged.
14. **An app update could leave an old yt-dlp in place for a week.** Updating the app puts the bundled yt-dlp back, and the weekly check did not know. It now checks again after each app update.
15. mpv's read-ahead caps were halved to 16 MiB each way, and the yt-dlp warm-up at launch was removed.

## Verification

- Unit tests pass: Android 125, Windows 54, with no failures.
- Android 14 emulator, final release APK: first play, seeks on normal and 3-hour tracks, skips, shared links, downloading a long track while it plays, and a read resuming after an idle close. An earlier build in this pass also covered downloading a played song and an unplayed song, and the Downloads screen, with no network request when replaying a download.
- Two rounds of random taps and swipes (450 and 600 gestures) with no crash or ANR. Stock `monkey` is not on the emulator image, so this was a small adb script. The first round ran on an earlier build, the second on the final APK.
- Fallback path, with the whole-song client disabled in a temporary build: the iOS link started the song in about 2 s, was refused on its second chunk, yt-dlp answered in 2.8 s, and the whole song buffered with no audible stop.
- The app installed over 2.14.27 (checked on the build before these last changes, signed with the same key).
- Windows `--selftest` passed, including deep seeks, mid-bar seeks and end-of-song advance. `--dltest` passed, including a song already saved and a 78 MB mix. The installer passes the Smart App Control `/HELP` check.

**Not tested:**
- A real phone or a mobile network. The emulator shares this PC's connection, and carrier networks may get YouTube's bot check more often.
- Playing offline with no connection at all.
- JioSaavn fallback on Android after these changes.
- Running the new installer over an installed app.

## Known risks

- **YouTube can change `VISIONOS` at any time.** When that happens the app falls back as above, which is slower but tested. Check `clientVersion` and the device fields in `Context.kt` against yt-dlp's `_base.py` when updating.
- The visitor id is kept for 6 hours and dropped as soon as YouTube refuses it.
- **This build keeps the number 2.14.26, so apps already on 2.14.26 are not offered it as an update.** Their users have to download it again and install over the old one. The next release should use a new number.

## Suggested next steps, most useful first

1. **Update the client settings without an app release.** Read the client name and version from a small JSON file on the website, so a YouTube change does not need a new APK and installer.
2. **Update the bundled yt-dlp on Windows.** The PC build still ships 2026.08.19 and now updates itself on first run. Bump the pin in `windows/build.gradle.kts` so a fresh install starts current.
3. **Remove the iOS fallback if `VISIONOS` stays reliable.** After a few weeks, `limitedStreams`, `DIRECT_STREAM_LIMIT` and the partial-link logic could go, and that part of the resolver gets much simpler.
4. **Move the Android resolver out of `PlayerService`.** It is a roughly 300-line closure with no direct tests. A separate class would match the PC's `StreamResolver` and could be tested.
5. **Sign the Windows build.** "Window ready" varied from 2.7 s to 51 s in the logs. Most of that is a cold JVM and Smart App Control scanning unsigned files, and code signing is the real fix.

## Re-audit and second fix pass (6 November 2026)

A second audit tested the published build on a fresh emulator. Seven bugs were reproduced there, and three were found by reading the Windows code; all ten are fixed below. Resume on reopen was added, as asked for. The fixes are re-issued as 2.14.26 again, with Android `versionCode` 2142900.

| # | App | Bug | Fix | Checked |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Android | After the app was killed, it came back on the wrong song: the position was saved, but the song only on queue changes | The queue is saved on every song change | Killed at 2:28, reopened on the same song |
| 2 | Both | An internet drop locked songs to JioSaavn (an hour on Android, a day on PC) | Silence from YouTube now counts as a connection problem: nothing is pinned, yt-dlp isn't started, and a JioSaavn stand-in is only used for the moment | 38 s outage: 0 pins, Python not loaded |
| 3 | Android | Slow recovery (30–60 s) after the connection came back, with no message; it checked "connected" rather than "has internet" | It waits with backoff (2, 4 then 8 s) and shows *Waiting for connection* in the player and mini player | Playing about 12 s after the network returned |
| 4 | Windows | Same as #2 | Same fix (`NoAnswerException`) | Code and build only |
| 5 | Windows | A download refused partway reused the refused link, then switched to JioSaavn | `StreamRefusedException` makes the retry get a new link | Unit-tested piece copy |
| 6 | Android | Changing the streaming quality could switch a partly saved song to JioSaavn | A partly saved song keeps the format it was saved in; a format change restarts at the same moment instead of switching source | Data saver to High: no switch, no pin |
| 7 | Android | Next on the last song stopped the music | It plays out the queue, so similar songs follow, as at a natural end | Queue grew from 15 to 63 and kept playing |
| 8 | Android | The next song was re-read from disk on every play/pause | Saved songs are remembered | 1 read across 3 pause/resume cycles |
| 9 | Windows | The second radio song wasn't saved ahead | Any queue change asks for the next song | Code |
| 10 | Website | FAQ named a setting that doesn't exist | Text fixed | Live site |

Also changed:
- **Resume on reopen, both apps.** If music was playing when the app closed, it carries on from the same moment; a paused song comes back paused but preloaded, so Play is instant. The position is saved every 5 s, and exactly on a normal close. On PC a start hidden in the tray stays quiet. Measured: Windows had sound 0.5–0.6 s after reopening, and Play to sound took about 0.28 s when paused; Android was playing again about 1.1 s after reopening (3.5 s just after an app update), and Play to playing took 0.4 s.
- **Mobile data (Android).** The player reads two minutes ahead instead of five, and saves only the first 512 KB of the next song. The emulator ignores the metered setting, so this rule is covered by `MobileDataRestTest` rather than run end to end.
- **Speed.** The visitor id is fetched at start, so the first song doesn't wait for it, and the media session shows a new song at once rather than after its length is known.
- **GitHub CI.** `gradlew` is now executable, and `UpdaterSignerTest` checks the JDK's own signed launcher instead of `cmd.exe`, which reports no signer on the runners.

Verified: unit tests (Android 127, Windows 54), lint, a 600-gesture random-tap run with no crash or ANR, Windows `--selftest`, `--dltest` and the new three-step `--resumetest`, and the Smart App Control installer check.

Still not tested: a real phone, real mobile data, and running the installer over an installed app.
